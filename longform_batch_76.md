# Long-Form Expansion Batch Seventy-Six

## Chapter One Hundred Twelve — Without the Name

The federation removed Skyler’s name from the library wall.

The plaque had been installed by a donor during the first year. It called him founder, witness, and architect of the Four Pillars.

The language was inaccurate.

He had helped begin the work, but he had not built it alone. He had also made decisions that others had repaired.

Mara proposed replacing the plaque with a list of everyone who had contributed during the library’s first year.

There were two hundred and nineteen names.

The donor objected. “That is not a memorial. That is a directory.”

“Then perhaps the directory is more honest,” Noor said.

The plaque came down.

For a week, the wall was blank.

Skyler passed it each morning and felt a small, embarrassing absence in his chest. He had believed he wanted no monument. He had not understood how much he wanted the monument to be removed by people who still thanked him.

The new installation contained four sentences instead of names:

**No record is complete.**

**No witness is neutral.**

**No consent is permanent.**

**No consequence belongs to one person.**

Beneath the sentences was a panel that could be updated every year.

The first annual list included cleaners, translators, drivers, childcare workers, volunteers who had left after disagreements, and two people who had publicly criticized the federation before later helping repair its policy.

Skyler’s name did not appear.

Someone asked whether this omission was another kind of dishonesty.

Noor said, “The archive contains his name. The wall does not need to.”

That evening, Skyler visited the library after closing. The four sentences were lit by a narrow strip of light.

He placed his hand against the blank space beside the panel.

He expected grief.

Instead, he felt a strange release. A legacy that required his name was not a legacy. It was a demand for continued presence.

The work had become capable of disappointing him.

That was how he knew it was alive.

Outside, the city moved through rain and reflected advertisements. The old machinery of influence continued somewhere beyond the windows. People still lied. Institutions still protected themselves. Technology still magnified whatever humans fed into it.

Nothing had been solved forever.

But the library’s doors opened without him.

The record corrected itself without his permission.

The children’s box remained in the closet.

The crown stayed in storage.

The name was absent from the wall.

The work remained.
