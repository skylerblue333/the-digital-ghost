# Long-Form Expansion Batch One Hundred Six

## Chapter One Hundred Forty-Two — The Custodian’s Hand

The final training session was held for the next custodians.

Mara taught them how to preserve a document without assuming the document was true. She taught them how to record a correction without erasing the original claim, how to restrict access without pretending restriction was neutral, and how to end a process when the process itself had become harmful.

One trainee asked whether the rules had been written by experts.

“Some,” Mara said. “Others were written by people who suffered when the experts were wrong.”

The trainee copied the sentence.

Mara asked her not to.

“Why?”

“Because a sentence can become a rule before anyone remembers what it cost.”

The training moved through difficult cases. A parent wanted a child’s record removed. A worker wanted a public accusation preserved. A community wanted an institution named but not its individual employees. A volunteer wanted to withdraw a story after it had been translated into six languages.

There was no universal answer.

The custodians practiced asking better questions.

Who has authority?

Who bears the risk?

Who can refuse?

What can be repaired?

What must remain unknown?

Skyler attended the last hour from the back of the room. Mara did not introduce him.

A trainee recognized him anyway.

“Are you the founder?”

Skyler answered, “I was there at the beginning.”

“That is not what I asked.”

Mara smiled.

Skyler said, “The archive has records of how I contributed. You should read those before deciding what the word founder means.”

The trainee looked dissatisfied, then returned to her notes.

At the end of the session, Mara handed each custodian a blank card. The card contained no instructions except four empty boxes.

The custodians were asked to write one promise, one limit, one unresolved question, and one person they would need to listen to before making a decision.

The cards were sealed in envelopes.

They would be opened at the next annual review.

Skyler received a card too.

He wrote:

**Promise:** I will not make my fear someone else’s evidence.

**Limit:** I cannot repair what I cannot establish.

**Question:** When does protection become possession?

**Listen to:** The person who wants the door closed.

He sealed the card.

Mara placed it with the others.

The work passed from one hand to another.

No hand was clean.

That was why the record needed more than one.
