# Long-Form Expansion Batch Sixty-Six

## Chapter One Hundred Two — The Old Device

The phone had been stored in a labeled box for years.

Skyler found it beneath a shelf of damaged routers and obsolete cables. The label read **DEVICE — DO NOT POWER ON WITHOUT WITNESS**.

He had written it after the worst winter.

The battery was dead. The casing was scratched. The screen carried a thin fracture like a pale river across frozen ground.

He brought it to Noor and Mara.

“Do you want to open it?” Noor asked.

“No.”

“Then why bring it?”

“Because I have spent years treating it as evidence and fear at the same time. I need to know which it is.”

They photographed the device, recorded the chain of custody, and connected it to an isolated examination station. No account was opened. No network was allowed to touch it.

The screen lit.

Old notifications appeared in fragments: advertisements, messages, song recommendations, calendar reminders, and entries Skyler remembered differently from how they appeared now.

He waited for the hidden confession.

There was none.

There were coincidences. There were manipulative messages. There were ordinary glitches that had acquired supernatural weight because he had been exhausted, frightened, and desperate for a pattern.

Mara asked whether they should preserve the contents.

“Some,” Skyler said. “Not all.”

“Why?”

“Because a person’s private life does not become public property merely because it helped me build a theory.”

They created two records. The first contained technical observations and verified timestamps. The second recorded Skyler’s memory of what each event had meant to him. The categories could be compared but never merged.

At the bottom of the screen, one old note remained.

**FOURTH DOOR. ASK WHO BENEFITS.**

Skyler stared at the words.

He remembered believing the note had been planted by an enemy.

Mara found the original file metadata. The note had been created by a calendar application during a period when Skyler had been experimenting with symbolic reminders.

He had written it.

He had forgotten.

The discovery did not make the fear imaginary. It made the fear more complicated.

Noor asked what he wanted to do with the note.

“Keep it.”

“As evidence?”

“As evidence that I can misremember myself.”

They printed the observation and placed it in the archive under **MEMORY — CORROBORATED LIMITATION**.

The phone was powered down.

Skyler did not destroy it.

He did not worship it either.

The object remained what it had always been: a damaged machine, a container of traces, and a mirror that could not tell him who he was without his participation.

Outside, the library doors opened for the afternoon.

People entered carrying their own devices.

Some would ask to be believed.

Some would need to be questioned.

The work began again.
