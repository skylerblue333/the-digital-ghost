# Long-Form Expansion Batch Fifteen

## Chapter Forty-Eight — The Old Reflex

The mistake began with a spreadsheet.

Skyler created it to track threats against the library. Dates, accounts, locations, repeated phrases, and requested actions. The sheet was meant to help volunteers avoid panic.

By midnight, it contained seventy-three names.

He had marked some as confirmed attackers, some as amplifiers, and some as “possible connectors.” The last category grew fastest.

Hanna found him at the kitchen table with the screen glowing against his face.

“You said you would not build a private enemy list.”

“This is a safety list.”

“Who decided the categories?”

“I did.”

She looked at the sheet.

“Then it is a private enemy list with better grammar.”

He closed the laptop.

“They are targeting people.”

“Some are.”

“Some?”

“You wrote that word.”

He opened the sheet again. The categories stared back at him. The old architecture had returned quietly, dressed as diligence.

Luna entered the kitchen.

“Are you working?”

“Yes.”

“Is it bad work?”

Skyler looked at the spreadsheet.

“I don’t know.”

She came closer. “Then stop until you know.”

The command was simple enough for a child and difficult enough for an adult.

He shut the laptop.

The next morning, he showed the sheet to Mara. She did not criticize him immediately. She asked who had consented to be listed.

“No one,” he said.

“Then archive it privately and delete the names.”

“What if we need them?”

“Then request the information through a process.”

He deleted the sheet.

The action felt like removing a weapon from his own hand.

For the rest of the day, he worked at the public desk and answered no questions about who was dangerous. He helped a woman correct a date. He printed a refusal form. He made coffee for the volunteers.

At closing, Hanna handed him the blank card from the children’s deck.

“What does it mean?” he asked.

“Today?”

“Yes.”

“It means you caught yourself.”

“That is not enough.”

“No,” she said. “But it is where enough begins.”
