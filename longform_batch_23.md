# Long-Form Expansion Batch Twenty-Three

## Chapter Fifty-Eight — The Splinter

The Society did not disappear. It subdivided.

One group called itself Continuity. They believed the old system had failed because it had become too visible. Another called itself Mercy, though its members demanded access to private records so they could “prevent future harm.” A third used no name at all and communicated through songs, edited photographs, and blank pages.

The public wanted Skyler to identify the central command.

There was no central command left.

Or perhaps there had never been one.

The splinter groups fought publicly and coordinated privately. They accused one another of manipulation while using the same language to recruit followers. Each claimed to be the last responsible keeper of the truth.

Lena brought Skyler a document from Continuity.

“They say Thorne was a rogue actor,” she said.

“Was he?”

“I don’t know.”

“Then why ask me?”

“Because you used to need a center.”

He looked at the document. It contained real names mixed with invented affiliations. The structure was designed to reward certainty.

Skyler had seen it before.

He knew the seduction of the clean map.

“Publish the method,” he said. “Not the list.”

Lena sighed. “You always choose the less dramatic answer.”

“It is usually the more accurate one.”

“Accuracy does not travel.”

“Then let it travel slowly.”

The splinter groups began targeting one another. A song accused Mercy of selling access. Continuity released a partial Ledger. Anonymous accounts claimed Skyler had secretly joined both.

He refused every interview that required him to name a master enemy.

Instead, he published a short statement:

**A fragmented system can still cause organized harm. The absence of a single leader does not make the harm imaginary. The presence of a convincing leader does not make every accusation true.**

The statement received little attention.

That night, Mara asked whether he regretted refusing the larger story.

“I regret how much people need one.”

“People need maps.”

“Then we make maps with blank areas.”

She looked at him.

“Blank areas are where people invent monsters.”

“Filled areas are where institutions hide them.”

Neither answer settled the matter.

They kept the blank areas visible.
