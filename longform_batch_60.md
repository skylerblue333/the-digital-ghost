# Long-Form Expansion Batch Sixty

## Chapter Ninety-Six — The Different Door

The federation’s first audit found that the member groups were not following the same rules.

One community published all corrections. Another kept names sealed. A third allowed anonymous challenges. A fourth required public testimony before changing a record.

The differences alarmed the funders.

“Your network lacks consistency,” they said.

Noor answered, “It has accountability.”

The auditors compared outcomes. Some local systems protected privacy better. Others corrected records faster. One created a backlog so severe that people stopped submitting requests.

The federation published the differences without ranking them.

Skyler wanted to recommend a single standard.

Imani asked, “Which failure do you want everyone to share?”

The question ended the argument.

Instead, the federation created a learning exchange. Each group documented its strongest practice and its worst failure. Communities could adopt changes, but they had to preserve the reason for the change and the record of disagreement.

The system remained uneven.

It also remained capable of learning.

At the closing meeting, a representative from the international group introduced a new symbol: five doors, none larger than the others.

“What does it mean?” Skyler asked.

“That no door is the center.”

He looked at the symbol for a long time.

The entire novel had begun with a person searching for the center of a hidden system. The answer had never been a room. It had been the desire to find one.

The federation did not end the Society.

It ended the need to imagine that every harm had one owner.

The work moved outward.

The doors multiplied.

No one held the key to all of them.
