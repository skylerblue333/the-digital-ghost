# Long-Form Expansion Batch Thirty-Nine

## Chapter Seventy-Five — The Year-End Record

At the end of the first year, Open Door published a report with fewer numbers than its funders wanted.

It counted records corrected, disputes opened, refusals preserved, and people who requested deletion. It did not count lives saved. It did not count trust restored. It did not count the number of visitors who returned because the library had become a place where their questions were not immediately turned into data.

“Those are the outcomes people understand,” the foundation representative said.

“We cannot prove them,” Skyler replied.

“You cannot prove anything human.”

“Then we should not pretend.”

The report included a section called **WHAT WE DO NOT KNOW**. It listed whether the project had reduced retaliation, whether public corrections changed private decisions, whether people felt safer, and whether the Society had adapted to the new systems.

The section was longer than the achievements.

The board wanted to shorten it.

The volunteers refused.

At the public meeting, a woman asked whether admitting uncertainty weakened the project.

“It makes the project accountable to reality,” Imani said.

A man in the back accused them of using uncertainty to avoid responsibility.

Skyler answered, “Uncertainty is not an excuse. It is a condition. We are responsible for what we claim despite it.”

The report passed.

It did not trend.

The library received three new volunteers and one anonymous donation with no conditions. Maria approved the donation only after the source agreed to public listing.

The girls drew a new card for the year-end archive.

The card showed a house with four windows. One window was dark. One showed a kitchen. One showed a library. The last showed only sky.

“What does it mean?” Skyler asked.

“Everything is part of the record,” Luna said.

“Even the dark window?”

“Especially if you can’t see inside.”

He placed the card in the archive.

The year ended without a final revelation.

The Society remained possible. The city remained imperfect. The work remained unfinished.

Skyler looked at the report and understood that a life could be meaningful without becoming complete.
