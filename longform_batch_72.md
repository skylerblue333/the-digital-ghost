# Long-Form Expansion Batch Seventy-Two

## Chapter One Hundred Eight — The Unfinished Record

The archive’s first anniversary was marked by an unfinished record.

Noor chose it deliberately.

The record concerned a person who had submitted a correction, withdrawn it, submitted it again, and then disappeared from the federation’s correspondence. There was no confirmed death, no forwarding address, and no permission to publish the story.

Only the uncertainty remained.

The anniversary program listed the case as **OPEN — NO PUBLIC NARRATIVE AUTHORIZED**.

Some visitors found the label unsatisfying.

A historian asked whether an archive that refused to tell a story was failing its purpose.

Mara answered, “Sometimes the purpose is to refuse the story until the person can own it.”

Skyler stood near the back wall, beneath a photograph of the first four doors.

He had once believed that every mystery became less frightening when named.

Now he understood that naming could become another kind of possession.

The federation read the available facts aloud. The person had challenged an identity record. A staff member had requested additional documentation. A volunteer had sent a reminder too many times. The person had replied with one sentence: **You keep asking me to prove that I am allowed to be myself.**

Then the correspondence stopped.

No one knew whether the person had found help elsewhere, abandoned the process, or decided that the archive was another institution that could not hear them.

The staff recorded their own failures.

They did not call the case resolved.

They did not turn the absent person into a symbol of resilience.

After the reading, a visitor asked Skyler whether the unfinished record haunted him.

“Yes.”

“What do you do with that?”

“I keep it from becoming entertainment.”

The visitor looked at the blank section of the display.

“Isn’t that what all archives do?”

“Some do. Ours should not.”

The answer was not a defense. It was a promise that could be broken.

That evening, the anniversary guests left small paper squares beneath the broken clock. On each square, they wrote what they had not been able to prove.

Some wrote names.

Some wrote apologies.

One person wrote only **I WAS HERE**.

Skyler added a square of his own.

**I AM STILL LEARNING THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN WITNESS AND OWNER.**

He placed it beneath the others and walked away without photographing it.

For once, the record did not need him to preserve it.
