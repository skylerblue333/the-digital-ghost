## *Loathing in the Woes of Ruthlessness: The Chosen One*

### Retail Positioning

**Primary genre:** Literary psychological techno-thriller.

**Secondary genres:** Speculative crime fiction, cyberwar thriller, psychological suspense, family drama.

**Manuscript length:** Approximately 22,000 words in the current concise novel/novella-length edition. It should be marketed accurately as a short novel or novella-length work, not as a full-length 75,000–90,000-word commercial novel.

**Tone:** Dark, cinematic, philosophical, emotionally intense, rebellious, and ultimately hopeful.

### Amazon Book Description

Skyler Blue built systems to protect vulnerable people.

Now his own life has become a system designed to destroy him.

After a devastating legal and family crisis, Skyler’s phone begins displaying impossible messages. Advertisements echo private memories. Songs appear to describe events before they happen. Political posts seem to speak directly to him. Then an anonymous rap battle erupts between rival American and Chinese hacker factions—and the lyrics begin naming details from Skyler’s life.

When a mysterious criminal network offers him twenty million dollars to become its digital operator, Skyler realizes the chaos may be more than surveillance. It may be an attempt to recruit him, frame him, or turn his pain into a public weapon.

The Pawns Network does not control everyone. It controls enough. It gives different people different stories until friends, employers, strangers, and institutions begin moving against the same target without knowing why.

With the help of a compromised hacker known as Ghost, an investigative journalist, and a federal investigator who refuses to give him easy answers, Skyler creates the Truth Protocol—a record separating what he knows from what he believes and what he fears.

But evidence is not the same as justice. Winning the story may still cost him his humanity.

To protect his daughters, Skyler must decide what kind of chosen one he will become: a weapon, a victim, a symbol—or a man willing to build something better after the war is over.

### Short Description

A father, a phone, and a hidden influence network collide in a dark literary techno-thriller about surveillance, betrayal, cyberwar propaganda, and the cost of becoming a symbol.

### Content and Positioning Note

The book should be presented as fiction. Avoid describing it in the retail listing as testimony, a verified account of government wrongdoing, or an allegation against real public figures or groups. The strongest commercial position is the novel’s actual premise: a morally complex father caught inside a fictional influence operation that weaponizes technology, media, and uncertainty.
