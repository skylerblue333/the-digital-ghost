# Long-Form Expansion Batch Thirty-Five

## Chapter Seventy-One — Equal Process

The accusation came from someone Open Door had helped.

A tenant named Corin claimed the project had corrected Priya’s record faster because Priya knew Skyler personally. The claim was not true, but the possibility was easy to imagine. Priya had been the first public case. Her story had received attention. Corin’s request had remained in review for six weeks.

The council met with both of them.

Corin did not want an apology. He wanted the process changed.

“You say every person is equal,” he said. “But some people arrive with better language, more time, and people who already believe them.”

Skyler could not argue.

The project had built an open door and assumed everyone could reach it.

They reviewed the intake records. Priya had submitted a clean request with supporting documents. Corin had sent messages from a phone with limited data, missed two appointments, and used language the volunteers had classified as aggressive.

The system had not officially punished him.

It had slowed him.

The difference mattered.

Ruth proposed a rotating advocate system. No one would be assigned an advocate without consent, but anyone could request help translating a record into the format the review process required.

Corin said, “That sounds like making people perform being understandable.”

Imani answered, “Then the process must accept more than one form of language.”

They created audio submissions, oral interviews, and a plain-language option. The changes required more labor. They also exposed how often “neutral” procedure favored people who had learned the institution’s vocabulary.

Skyler wrote a public apology.

**We treated equal access as if it meant identical instructions. We were wrong. Equal process requires recognizing unequal conditions.**

The statement attracted criticism from both sides. Some said the project had become political. Others said it had not gone far enough.

Corin returned three months later with a corrected record.

He did not thank them.

He said, “Now the door is closer to where people actually stand.”

Skyler considered that the highest compliment the project could earn.
