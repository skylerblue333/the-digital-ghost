# Long-Form Expansion Batch Twenty-Eight

## Chapter Sixty-Three — The Ordinary Life

The girls stopped asking whether the phone was watching.

They began asking whether Skyler was.

The question came after he photographed the kitchen table before leaving for the library. He wanted a record of the card game they had invented. He wanted to remember the four blank cards, the crooked strawberries, the green door drawn in marker.

Luna took the camera from his hand.

“Why did you take that?”

“To preserve it.”

“Preserve what?”

“The game.”

“You were here.”

“Yes.”

“Then you remember.”

He looked at the photograph. The image contained the table, the cards, and part of Summer’s sleeve.

“Sometimes I forget.”

“That does not mean you get to keep everything.”

The sentence came from somewhere beyond her age. He wondered whether she had heard it from Hanna or invented it alone.

“I’m sorry,” he said.

He deleted the photograph.

Summer watched the screen. “Is it gone?”

“From the camera.”

“Where else?”

He stopped.

The question belonged to the world he had helped build.

“Maybe nowhere,” he said. “Maybe somewhere I cannot see.”

Alexis placed the blank card over the other cards.

“Then don’t take pictures.”

For the next month, Skyler left the camera at the library. He wrote descriptions of family moments only when the girls agreed. Sometimes they said no. Sometimes they said yes but changed their minds later.

The record became smaller.

The life became larger.

At school, the girls had a project about local history. They asked Skyler to speak about Aegis.

He said no.

Not because the history did not matter. Because their classroom did not belong to his story.

Instead, he helped them build a model of the neighborhood with cardboard, paint, and string. Four doors appeared in the model. A bridge connected the church to the library. A blank space represented the places they had not visited.

“What does the blank mean?” the teacher asked.

Luna answered, “It means we do not know yet.”

The teacher smiled.

Skyler stood behind the children and felt the old hunger for recognition move through him.

He wanted to say that they had learned from him.

He said nothing.

The lesson belonged to them.
