# Long-Form Expansion Batch Thirty-Four

## Chapter Seventy — The Inquiry

The city inquiry began with a question no one wanted to answer.

Was the key a threat?

The police called it suspicious. The security guard called it a warning. A reporter called it a symbol. Skyler called it an object whose meaning had not yet been proven.

The committee chair looked annoyed.

“Mr. Spillers, people are asking whether your organization is under coordinated attack.”

“Some incidents appear coordinated.”

“That is not what I asked.”

“It is the most accurate answer I have.”

The chair glanced at the cameras. “You have made uncertainty a brand.”

Skyler felt the room tighten.

The phrase would travel well. It was sharp enough to become a headline. He wanted to answer with equal force.

Instead, he described the envelope, the key, the rear door, and the steps the library had taken. He said what had been observed. He said what remained unknown.

A committee member asked whether he believed the Society had sent it.

“I have no basis to say that.”

“Given your history, do you believe the threat is connected to the wider campaign?”

“I believe the question is reasonable. I do not believe the answer has been established.”

The public hearing became less dramatic than expected.

No one announced a secret network. No one revealed a master plan. The city recommended a safety audit and a liaison for future incidents.

Outside, the reporter who had accused Skyler of branding uncertainty waited beneath the steps.

“You had a chance to make them listen,” she said.

“I did.”

“You made yourself sound unsure.”

“I was unsure.”

“People do not follow uncertainty.”

“Then they should not follow me.”

She stared at him.

The sentence was not humility. It was a refusal to become a leader by pretending to know more than he did.

Later, the committee sent its report. The incident was classified as a credible concern but not linked to the larger campaign without further evidence.

The wording was dull.

Skyler pinned it beside the blank door.

Accuracy rarely looked heroic on paper.

That was one reason people kept choosing myths.
