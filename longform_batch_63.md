# Long-Form Expansion Batch Sixty-Three

## Chapter Ninety-Nine — The Empty Chair

The federation scheduled its first leadership election in a room with one empty chair.

Noor placed it at the center of the circle.

Skyler understood the gesture immediately.

“Is that for me?” he asked.

“No,” she said. “It is for whoever is not here yet.”

The members laughed, but uneasily.

For months, people had treated Skyler as the federation’s origin, conscience, and emergency brake. He had become the person journalists called when a record failed. His name appeared in essays about post-surveillance ethics. A university invited him to speak about the burden of evidence.

He declined the invitation.

The burden did not belong to him alone.

During the election, a member accused him of hiding behind humility.

“You want power,” she said. “You just want the moral credit for refusing it.”

The accusation struck harder than the others because it contained enough truth to hurt.

Skyler had learned to distrust crowns. He had not learned to distrust the desire to be seen refusing one.

He stood.

“I wanted to be the person who could not be replaced,” he said. “That desire made me dangerous. I used to call it responsibility because responsibility sounded better.”

Noor watched him without rescue in her face.

He continued.

“I still want to be necessary. I am not going to pretend otherwise. But a system that depends on my goodness is not a safe system.”

The election created four rotating roles: witness, custodian, challenger, and translator. No role could be held by the same person twice in succession. Every decision required a public rationale and a recorded objection period.

Skyler received no permanent title.

He was appointed to the temporary archive committee for ninety days.

The work was tedious. It involved mislabeled files, missing timestamps, duplicate reports, and arguments about whether a correction could be called a correction when the original statement had never been proven.

He loved the work.

It offered no applause.

One night, while organizing the archive, he found a blank form with his name already printed at the top.

**PRIMARY WITNESS: SKYLER BLUE SPILLERS**

He tore the form in half.

Then he stopped.

Instead of destroying it, he marked the field **UNCONFIRMED**, scanned the form, and added it to the record.

The old instinct had been to erase the story that threatened him.

The new discipline was to show how the story had been made.

The empty chair remained in the center of the room.

Nobody sat in it.

That was the point.
