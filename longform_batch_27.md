# Long-Form Expansion Batch Twenty-Seven

## Chapter Sixty-Two — Imani’s Testimony

Imani testified in a room where no one knew how to pronounce her name.

The hearing concerned the original Aegis contracts. The board had dissolved, but its members had become consultants, investors, and advisors to new companies. None believed they had built the Society. All believed they had made reasonable decisions under pressure.

Imani brought the refusal logs.

She also brought the meeting minutes from the day the board voted to remove them.

The chair asked whether the system had been designed to manipulate people.

“No,” Imani said.

The answer disappointed the room.

“It was designed to coordinate identity records,” she continued. “Manipulation entered through the choices people made about which records mattered, which refusals were inconvenient, and which predictions deserved authority.”

“Who made those choices?”

“Everyone who accepted a benefit without examining the cost.”

The chair frowned. “That is too broad to be useful.”

“It is broad because the system was broad.”

A former board member accused her of rewriting history.

“You left,” he said. “You abandoned the project and now claim moral superiority.”

Imani looked at him.

“I did not abandon the project because I was better than you. I left because I was afraid I would become more like you if I stayed.”

The room quieted.

She described the moment the board removed the refusal layer. The vote had been unanimous except for hers. The language had called refusal logs inefficient, duplicative, and vulnerable to abuse.

“Did you know what would happen?” the chair asked.

“I knew what could happen.”

“Why did you not expose it?”

“Because I believed a collapse would harm the people already inside the system.”

“Was that true?”

“Partly.”

“And the rest?”

Imani looked at the public gallery where Skyler sat with Mara.

“The rest was fear. I wanted to be right without becoming responsible for what followed.”

After the hearing, Skyler found her outside beneath the courthouse awning.

“You told them the whole thing.”

“No.”

He waited.

“I told them the part I could support. The rest is still memory.”

He smiled.

“Did you learn that from us?”

“No. I learned it from failing.”

Rain moved along the curb.

Skyler asked whether she thought Aegis could ever be forgiven.

Imani looked at the courthouse.

“Systems are not forgiven. People are. Sometimes.”

“Do you forgive yourself?”

“Not as a conclusion.”

The answer stayed with him.

Forgiveness as a conclusion could become another form of closure. Forgiveness as a practice required returning to the record without demanding that the record become kind.
