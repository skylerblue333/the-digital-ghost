# Long-Form Expansion Batch Forty-Nine

## Chapter Eighty-Five — The Gatekeeper

The accusation appeared on the wall before the meeting began.

**OPEN DOOR IS STILL A GATE.**

Someone had written it in charcoal beneath the four painted doors. The letters were large enough for every visitor to read.

Noor wanted to clean it.

Skyler stopped her.

“Leave it until the meeting.”

“What if people think it is true?”

“What if it is?”

The meeting filled the library. The person who had written the words did not identify themselves. Instead, they sent a statement through a friend.

The statement said Open Door had become a gatekeeper because it decided which records were public, which claims were verified, and which people deserved access to the archive. It said the project had replaced one system of control with another, more polite system.

Skyler read the statement aloud.

No one interrupted.

When he finished, he said, “Some of this is true.”

The room shifted.

“We decide what we publish. We decide what we preserve. We decide which requests meet the process. That is authority, even when we use it carefully.”

A volunteer asked whether the project should dissolve.

Imani said, “Dissolution would not remove the need for the functions. It would only remove the current people.”

A tenant asked whether authority could ever be harmless.

“No,” Skyler said. “The goal is not harmlessness. The goal is answerability.”

The word was not satisfying.

It was still the best one available.

They proposed a public access audit: every decision to restrict, redact, delay, or preserve would be reviewed by people outside the council. The auditors would have their own recall process. Their names and terms would be public.

The system became slower again.

The charcoal remained on the wall for one week.

A visitor added a second line beneath it.

**THEN SHOW US THE GATE.**

Skyler looked at the words before opening the library.

He thought of the blank door, the hidden rooms, the screens that had once turned people into profiles. The worst systems were not always those with gates.

They were the systems that denied the gates existed.

He unlocked the front door.

The public entered.
