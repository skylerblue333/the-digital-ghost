# Long-Form Expansion Batch Forty-Eight

## Chapter Eighty-Four — The Missing Card

The missing card was discovered by accident.

A box in the refusal archive contained three hundred and ninety-nine cards instead of four hundred. The gap appeared between a public request and its response. No one remembered removing it.

The card had belonged to an unnamed person who had refused a predictive profile. The only remaining record said **REQUEST RECEIVED**.

No response.

Ghost wanted to search every backup.

Skyler stopped him.

“We do not know that the card was stolen.”

“It is missing.”

“Missing is an observation.”

“Who else would remove it?”

The question carried the old hunger for a culprit.

They reviewed the transfer logs. The box had been moved three times. One volunteer remembered seeing the card. Another remembered a blank space. A third said the box had arrived that way.

The absence became a story before it became evidence.

Noor proposed a public notice asking whether anyone recognized the record. Skyler worried that the notice would expose the person if they were still alive.

They published a neutral statement:

**One archive item is missing. We are seeking information about the record’s movement without publishing its content.**

A week later, an envelope arrived.

Inside was the card.

On the front, the person had written:

**I TOOK IT BACK.**

On the back:

**YOU CANNOT PRESERVE MY REFUSAL BY KEEPING IT FROM ME.**

The council invited the person to decide what should happen next. They declined to identify themselves. They asked only that the archive record the return without reproducing the card’s wording.

The card was placed in a sealed envelope with the person’s own statement.

The archive count became four hundred again.

The missing space remained in the public index.

Not as a mystery.

As a reminder that preservation was not ownership.

Skyler added a line to the Living Draft:

**A record belongs first to the person whose life it describes.**

He knew the sentence would create problems.

That was one reason it belonged there.
