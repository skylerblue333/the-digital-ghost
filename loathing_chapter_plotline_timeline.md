# *Loathing in the Woes of Ruthlessness: The Chosen One*
## Chapter Plotline and Master Timeline

> **Fictional framing:** This outline treats the Pawns Network, the cyberwar, the manipulated media, the criminal operations, and the psychological attacks as fictional elements of a speculative thriller. The story preserves the protagonist’s emotional experience while keeping the central mystery open: some events are demonstrably engineered, some are criminal exploitation, and some may be Skyler’s frightened interpretation of coincidence.

## Story Promise

Skyler Blue is a gifted coder, father, and former founder of a social-impact technology company. After a personal and legal catastrophe, his phone begins presenting advertisements, songs, messages, and recommendations that appear to know his private life. An anonymous rap battle between rival American and Chinese hacker factions seems to narrate his downfall in real time. Criminal intermediaries offer him a lucrative position operating inside the dark web. Employers, friends, romantic partners, and strangers appear to behave as if they have been given different versions of the same secret story.

The organization behind the disturbance is the **Pawns Network**, a decentralized influence machine built on one principle: *You do not need to control a person when you can control everyone around them.* It does not control everything. It controls enough uncertainty to make the victim doubt the difference.

The novel’s central question is not simply who hacked Skyler. It is whether a person can regain moral agency after his memories, reputation, relationships, and perception have all become contested territory.

## The Pawns Network

The Pawns Network is not a single omnipotent cabal. It is a fictional criminal influence brokerage that sells **narrative pressure**. Its brokers distribute different claims to different people. One employee is told Skyler is a criminal. Another is told he is an informant. A third believes she is protecting him. A fourth is paid to provoke him. The Network’s power comes from fragmentation.

The Network uses compromised accounts, paid intermediaries, data brokers, staged rumors, selective leaks, synthetic media, financial pressure, and social manipulation. The technical details remain impressionistic rather than instructional. The thriller focuses on consequences: an altered feed, an unexpected song, a vanished job offer, a stranger repeating a private phrase, or a false file that seems designed to make Skyler look guilty.

The Network has three divisions. **The Choir** manufactures music, memes, and political spectacle. **The Ledger** handles payments, blackmail, and criminal logistics. **The Mirror Room** profiles targets and designs the sequence of coincidences that makes a target feel surrounded.

# Part I — THE GLITCH

## Chapter 1 — The Phone

**Time:** Monday, August 10, 2026, 6:12 a.m.–11:40 p.m.

Skyler wakes before dawn to a phone notification that uses a phrase he spoke only in the shower the previous night. He dismisses it as algorithmic coincidence. At breakfast, an advertisement appears for a vehicle matching one he once drove. A song in a convenience store seems to describe an argument from that morning. His friend Caleb jokes that the phone is listening.

That evening, an unknown number sends: **YOU FINALLY NOTICED.** A second message follows: **DON’T TRUST THE PEOPLE HELPING YOU.** Skyler screenshots both messages, but the screenshots later show a different timestamp.

**Purpose:** Establish the phone as an apparently haunted object and introduce the uncertainty between surveillance and pattern recognition.

**End beat:** A new audio file appears on Skyler’s device without a sender. It contains the sound of his own front door opening.

## Chapter 2 — The Year That Would Not End

**Time:** Tuesday, August 11.

The audio file triggers memories of 2020: courtrooms, custody arguments, accusations, and the sensation that his future had been decided by people who never knew him. The narrative shifts into close first person as Skyler explains how a man can survive an accusation and still remain imprisoned by it.

His mother Maria warns him not to turn every coincidence into evidence. Skyler hears this as criticism, then recognizes that she is afraid of losing him to the investigation.

**Purpose:** Define the wound and show that Skyler’s greatest vulnerability is not his phone but his unfinished grief.

**End beat:** A court document from 2020 appears in his cloud folder under a filename created that morning.

## Chapter 3 — The First Beat

**Time:** Wednesday, August 12.

A diss track titled **RED, WHITE, SILENT** begins circulating. Its verses mock an unnamed Chinese cyber faction. Hours later, an answer track titled **THE EAGLE HAS TWO SHADOWS** appears. Both tracks include veiled references to Skyler’s daughters, his former company, and the shelved Aegis platform.

Skyler contacts Lena Ortiz, an investigative journalist he once met at a technology conference. She agrees to speak only if he separates facts from theories.

**Purpose:** Introduce the rap battle as propaganda, communication, and psychological weapon.

**End beat:** The final bar of the second track says, “The father is listening.”

## Chapter 4 — The Drop

**Time:** Thursday, August 13.

A stranger approaches Skyler outside a laundromat and gives him a paper receipt containing a vehicle description, a color, a time, and a warehouse district. The stranger calls it a “drop” and says Skyler has already been paid. Skyler denies involvement in any criminal transaction.

He does not go to the location. Instead, he sends the receipt to Lena and Director Reyes. That night, a news alert reports that a vehicle matching the description was found abandoned near a warehouse connected to counterfeit currency investigators.

**Purpose:** Bring the organized-crime atmosphere into the plot without making Skyler a willing criminal participant.

**End beat:** Thorne calls from a blocked number and says, “You were never supposed to see the car.”

## Chapter 5 — The Offer

**Time:** Friday, August 14.

Thorne meets Skyler in a closed factory used by the Pawns Network’s Ledger division. He offers twenty million dollars, a new identity, protection, and a position as the Network’s “dark web operator.” The work is described in euphemisms: managing leaks, moving reputational pressure, and coordinating anonymous campaigns.

Thorne claims Skyler has misunderstood the chaos. According to him, no one is attacking Skyler; they are recruiting him because he understands identity systems better than anyone else available.

Skyler refuses. Thorne threatens to make Skyler appear responsible for crimes he did not commit and to turn every relationship in his life into evidence against him.

**End beat:** The lights fail. Ghost steps from the shadows and says, “He’s lying about one thing. They already started.”

# Part II — THE WAR NOBODY CAN SEE

## Chapter 6 — Ghost in the Factory

**Time:** Friday night into Saturday morning.

Ghost, revealed as Mara Vale, helps Skyler escape. She refuses to explain whether the gun she carries is real or theatrical until a shot from outside shatters a window. Rather than kill Thorne, Mara photographs him and uploads a limited identity packet to the Truth Protocol.

The escape is cinematic but costly: Skyler loses his phone, Mara is wounded, and the upload exposes only enough information to make Thorne dangerous rather than vulnerable.

**Purpose:** Establish Mara as an ally who is also compromised and morally culpable.

**End beat:** The Truth Protocol receives a message from an unknown administrator: **YOU HAVE PUBLISHED THE BAIT.**

## Chapter 7 — The Feed

**Time:** Saturday, August 15.

Skyler obtains a temporary device. Its recommendations quickly become saturated with the same topics: China, American politics, hackers, crime, fathers losing children, and songs about betrayal. He begins recording the screen rather than trusting screenshots.

Lena explains that personalized feeds can create the feeling of a coordinated attack without a single person controlling every post. Mara says the distinction does not comfort her because the Network knows how to exploit ordinary systems.

**Purpose:** Explore technology as both tool and amplifier of fear.

**End beat:** A recommendation appears for a video titled **SKYLER BLUE: THE MAN BEHIND THE DROP**, although Skyler has never publicly been associated with the warehouse.

## Chapter 8 — The Trump Notes

**Time:** Sunday, August 16.

Political posts apparently connected to a famous former president begin appearing around Skyler’s accounts. He shares several, then deletes them, then cannot determine which actions were his. The story avoids claiming that the public figure personally contacted him; the uncertainty lies in the use of impersonation, automation, and political spectacle.

Skyler realizes that the Network wants him to become a visible partisan symbol. The more he posts, the easier it becomes to classify him, discredit him, and recruit people to oppose him.

**Purpose:** Show how ideology becomes a trap and how the protagonist’s need to be heard can be weaponized.

**End beat:** Lena finds that three of Skyler’s posts were scheduled before he remembers writing them.

## Chapter 9 — The Pawns Network

**Time:** Monday, August 17.

Mara explains the Network’s structure. It does not control everyone. It gives different people different stories. One thinks Skyler is a criminal. One thinks he is an informant. One thinks she is rescuing him. The Network profits from the collision.

Skyler creates a wall of names, claims, and events. Lena forces him to mark each entry as **confirmed**, **possible**, or **unknown**.

**Purpose:** Introduce the novel’s central investigative discipline.

**End beat:** A name on Skyler’s wall appears in four unrelated categories, suggesting that someone inside his support circle is feeding the Network information.

## Chapter 10 — The Convenience Store

**Time:** Tuesday, August 18.

Skyler enters a convenience store. A song begins playing that appears to describe his morning. A different song from a passing car repeats the subject. His temporary phone recommends a video on the same topic. Caleb whispers, “They’re everywhere.”

Skyler asks who. Caleb does not answer. The phone buzzes: **YOU FINALLY NOTICED.** Then Ghost sends: **THAT’S EXACTLY WHAT THEY WANT.**

The sequence can be read two ways: an engineered psychological operation or a terrifying convergence of ordinary recommendation systems and heightened attention.

**Purpose:** Deliver the novel’s signature psychological-terror scene.

**End beat:** Skyler sees a stranger wearing the same cracked-mirror symbol that appears on the rap tracks.

# Part III — THE DIGITAL GHOST

## Chapter 11 — Seven Jobs

**Time:** Wednesday, August 19–Friday, August 28.

Skyler’s employment history is presented through short, escalating scenes: a contract withdrawn, an interview canceled, a supervisor repeating language from an anonymous post, a security review that finds no actionable violation but still ends his access. The number seven becomes a motif rather than a literal claim about every job.

He starts sleeping in short intervals and mistakes exhaustion for revelation. Hanna returns to his life and tells him, “A record can protect you, but it can also become a room with no door.”

**Purpose:** Show the psychological cost of constant fighting and the danger of making identity equal investigation.

**End beat:** Skyler discovers that someone has created a fake version of the Truth Protocol using his name.

## Chapter 12 — The Evidence

**Time:** Saturday, August 29.

Skyler, Lena, Mara, and Reyes establish a secure evidence process. The novel deliberately keeps the technical method high-level: preserve originals, record dates, compare independent sources, and avoid public accusations without corroboration.

Skyler begins distinguishing what happened from what he thinks it means. This is the first time he admits that his interpretation could be wrong without making his fear unreal.

**Purpose:** Turn the protagonist from reactive victim into disciplined investigator.

**End beat:** One entry marked “unknown” is independently confirmed by a financial record, proving that at least part of the operation is real.

## Chapter 13 — The Ledger

**Time:** September 1–September 4.

Lena traces the counterfeit-money drop to a chain of shell companies and paid couriers. The criminals are not a national government; they are opportunists using the cyberwar narrative as cover. The rap battle is financed by several groups that pretend to be enemies because conflict attracts attention.

Skyler is tempted to break into a compromised system to expose them. Reyes warns that unauthorized access could destroy the investigation and make Skyler the story.

**Purpose:** Bring the criminal plot into focus and confront Skyler with the boundary between justice and vigilantism.

**End beat:** The Ledger contains a payment labeled **CHOSEN ONE / PHASE FOUR**.

## Chapter 14 — The Mirror Room

**Time:** September 5–September 9.

Mara takes Skyler to an abandoned behavioral-research office once used by the Network’s Mirror Room. The walls contain profiles of targets, not magical omniscience but mundane fragments: public posts, purchases, relationships, court records, workplace conflicts, and emotional triggers.

Skyler recognizes details he never posted. Mara confesses that she once helped build the profiling system and that she contacted him years earlier as part of an experiment.

**Purpose:** Make the betrayal personal and reveal that Ghost has not been a pure rescuer.

**End beat:** Mara’s profile of Skyler ends with a prediction: **He will choose revenge when offered justice.**

## Chapter 15 — The Twenty-Million-Dollar Choice

**Time:** September 10.

Thorne makes the offer again, this time through a livestream that appears on screens across the city. He promises money, safety, and access to the files that could restore Skyler’s reputation. The price is to become the Network’s public symbol: a supposedly redeemed hacker who will redirect attention toward selected enemies.

Skyler nearly accepts when Thorne shows a photograph of his daughters at a public event. Lena proves the photograph was taken from an open source, but the threat remains emotionally effective.

**Purpose:** Test whether Skyler values truth or merely vindication.

**End beat:** Skyler says no, but his voice is captured and edited into a confession.

# Part IV — THE WAR

## Chapter 16 — The Two Flags

**Time:** September 11–September 13.

The American and Chinese diss tracks release their final verses. Each accuses the other of manipulating Skyler. The tracks contain identical background tones and the same four-note sequence.

Mara recognizes the sequence as a signal used by the Choir. The apparent national rivalry is partly a performance designed to convert political fear into clicks, donations, recruitment, and cover for criminal transactions.

**Purpose:** Reveal that the visible conflict is not the whole conflict.

**End beat:** A third, uncredited track plays the same four notes and says, “You were never fighting countries. You were fighting the story.”

## Chapter 17 — The Third Player

**Time:** September 14–September 18.

Evidence points toward the Pawns Network, but the Network is itself divided. Thorne controls the public narrative; the Ledger brokers control money; the Choir wants spectacle; the Mirror Room wants behavioral data. Each faction has been manipulating the others.

Skyler learns that no one possesses complete control. The terror came from the collision of partial control systems.

**Purpose:** Prevent an implausible all-powerful villain and deepen the theme of distributed responsibility.

**End beat:** Thorne orders an operation against his own allies, making the Network more dangerous because it is beginning to collapse.

## Chapter 18 — Everyone Lied

**Time:** September 19–September 22.

Mara admits she withheld the identity of the original client who commissioned Skyler’s profile. Lena reveals that her editor delayed publication because the newspaper accepted money from a Network-linked foundation. Reyes confesses that his office has been pressured to classify Skyler as a destabilizing threat rather than a victim.

Skyler experiences the revelation as total betrayal. Instead of abandoning the evidence process, he narrows it: each person must state exactly what they did, what they were told, and what they assumed.

**Purpose:** Replace the fantasy of pure allies and pure enemies with moral complexity.

**End beat:** The original client is not Thorne. It is someone connected to Skyler’s old Aegis project.

## Chapter 19 — The Truth Protocol

**Time:** September 23–September 27.

Skyler publishes a carefully bounded dossier. It contains verified financial links, documented threats, authenticated recordings, and a separate section labeled **UNRESOLVED**. He refuses to publish private details about uninvolved people, including his daughters and former partners.

The publication is attacked immediately. Some critics call it fabricated. Others exaggerate it into proof of a global conspiracy. Skyler realizes that even truthful evidence can be weaponized by people who want a larger myth.

**Purpose:** Define truth as restraint, not merely exposure.

**End beat:** The dossier triggers a hidden failsafe in Aegis, opening a file marked **FOURTH DOOR**.

## Chapter 20 — The Fourth Door

**Time:** September 28–October 2.

The file reveals that Aegis was designed around four principles: identity, memory, consent, and consequence. Skyler had unknowingly built an architecture that could map how people become vulnerable when identity and memory are manipulated.

The Pawns Network wanted the system not to control phones, but to predict which social pressures would make a person act against his own interests. Skyler sees that the true weapon is not surveillance alone. It is the ability to make a person feel that resistance is impossible.

**Purpose:** Connect the technology to the philosophical conflict.

**End beat:** The system identifies the original client as a consortium of legitimate firms and criminals operating through intermediaries, not a single government or secret society.

# Part V — THE CHOSEN ONE

## Chapter 21 — Thorne’s Secret

**Time:** October 3–October 5.

Skyler confronts Thorne in a public place where neither man can safely use violence. Thorne admits Skyler was chosen because he was visible, emotionally credible, technically literate, and already wounded by institutions. He could be turned into a hero, a criminal, or a martyr.

Thorne insists that he gave Skyler a purpose. Skyler answers that a purpose imposed through fear is only another form of ownership.

**Purpose:** State the novel’s central conflict directly: power versus humanity.

**End beat:** Thorne reveals that the final operation will use Skyler’s daughters as symbols in a public disinformation event, not as physical targets. The threat is reputational and psychological, but Skyler treats it as urgent.

## Chapter 22 — The Final Song

**Time:** October 6.

The final rap track appears simultaneously on several platforms. It uses voices from both supposed factions and contains fragments of earlier songs. The track does not name a country or organization. It asks whether a person can remain free when every choice is predicted.

Ghost identifies the hidden structure, but Skyler refuses to release it until Lena verifies the interpretation. Their discipline prevents a false accusation.

**Purpose:** Resolve the rap battle as a narrative weapon and a coded confession.

**End beat:** The track ends with a recording of Skyler saying, “I am the Chosen One,” though he has no memory of saying it.

## Chapter 23 — The Children

**Time:** October 7–October 10.

Skyler stops trying to win the internet. He focuses on protecting his daughters from becoming evidence, symbols, or leverage. He apologizes to Maria, Hanna, and Lena for treating their love as proof of loyalty and their hesitation as betrayal.

This is the emotional climax. Skyler understands that survival has made him suspicious of everyone, including the people who never claimed to be part of the war.

**Purpose:** Complete the internal transformation from isolated fighter to responsible father and collaborator.

**End beat:** Skyler chooses not to publish a damaging file that would destroy an innocent person’s life, even though publishing it would make his case appear stronger.

## Chapter 24 — The Choice

**Time:** October 11–October 14.

Skyler, Mara, Lena, and Reyes release the final evidence through multiple independent channels. They do not announce a grand conspiracy. They publish the verified criminal network, the financial mechanisms, the manipulated campaigns, the institutional failures, and the limits of what they can prove.

Thorne is arrested through a lawful process, but many Network members escape. The Pawns Network is damaged, not magically erased.

Skyler rejects both the role of obedient victim and the role of righteous hacker-king. He chooses to build a public-interest digital identity project with strict consent controls and independent oversight.

**Purpose:** Answer what the Chosen One chooses to become.

**End beat:** A new anonymous message arrives: **THE NETWORK IS GONE. THE PAWNS REMAIN.** Skyler deletes the message without opening the attachment.

## Epilogue — What Remains

**Time:** Six months later.

Skyler works from a modest office connected to a community technology center. The Truth Protocol has become a nonprofit archive staffed by journalists, advocates, and legal professionals. Skyler still notices patterns, but he no longer treats every pattern as a command.

His daughters draw four doors on a piece of paper. One is locked, one is open, one is burning, and one is blank. Skyler asks which door they want him to choose. They answer, “The one you build.”

A final song plays from a passing car. This time, Skyler does not assume it is about him. He watches the car disappear beneath the morning sky.

> **Final thematic statement:** Survival created the warrior. Purpose decided what the warrior became.

# Continuity Rules for Drafting

The story must never present a real government, public figure, ethnic group, or political party as secretly responsible without fictional transformation and narrative evidence. The American and Chinese factions should remain invented collectives whose identities are contested; the conflict is about propaganda and criminal opportunism rather than a claim about real-world populations.

Skyler must not become an all-knowing hacker. He can preserve evidence, recognize systems, and reason about patterns, but major conclusions require corroboration from Lena, Mara, Reyes, or independent records. His fear may be valid even when his interpretation is wrong.

The daughters remain emotionally important but are not used as disposable victims. Threats involving them should be reputational, symbolic, or protective dilemmas rather than graphic violence. The narrative should show consequences without glamorizing trafficking, counterfeit money, unlawful access, stalking, or vigilantism.

Ghost must remain morally complicated. Her revelations should help Skyler while also exposing her past participation. Thorne should be frightening because he understands incentives and narrative control, not because he possesses supernatural reach.

The ending must not claim that every mystery is solved. It should establish what was proven, what remains possible, and what Skyler chooses to do despite uncertainty.
