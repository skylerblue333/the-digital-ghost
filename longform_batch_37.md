# Long-Form Expansion Batch Thirty-Seven

## Chapter Seventy-Three — The Blackout

The power failed during the first snow.

The library went dark at 6:41 p.m., just as the evening review began. The backup lights came on for eleven seconds and then died. The computers shut down. The heaters stopped. Outside, the streetlights remained bright.

Someone had cut the line to the building.

Or the storm had done it.

The old fear returned before the explanation.

Ghost moved toward the rear entrance. The security guard stopped him.

“Visible route,” he said. “No one goes alone.”

They used flashlights. Volunteers guided visitors toward the front. Skyler carried the paper intake forms from the archive room.

A woman asked whether the records were safe.

“They are offline,” he said.

“Is that safer?”

“Not always.”

The answer did not help.

In the darkness, the building became a body without memory. Every corridor sounded different. Every door carried a possibility.

Skyler felt the urge to call Thorne, though Thorne had not spoken to him in months. The urge was not logical. It came from the part of him that believed the person who had designed the room might know how to turn off the dark.

He did not call.

The security guard led everyone outside in groups. Snow covered the sidewalk. The city’s bright streets made the powerless library look abandoned.

At 7:12, the power returned.

The computers rebooted. The archive checksums matched. No files were missing.

The utility company reported damage to a line caused by falling ice.

The explanation was ordinary.

Skyler believed it.

He also documented the outage.

Observed: power failed.  
Corroborated: utility damage.  
Inferred: none.  
Unknown: whether anyone anticipated the failure.

Ruth read the entry.

“Why keep the unknown?” she asked.

“Because later someone may learn something.”

“Or because you cannot let anything end.”

He looked at the dark windows.

“That too.”

The library reopened the next morning.

On the wall, someone had taped a card from the children’s deck. The drawing showed a black square with a small yellow dot in the center.

“What does it mean?” Skyler asked Luna.

“The light that was still there.”

He left the card where it was.
