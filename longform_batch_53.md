# Long-Form Expansion Batch Fifty-Three

## Chapter Eighty-Nine — The Workshop

Skyler returned to the workshop in late spring.

The building had been sold twice since he left. The sign above the door had lost another letter. Inside, the machines were covered in dust and plastic. The old printer remained in the corner where he had first heard the song beneath the static.

He had spent years treating the room as a crime scene.

Now it looked like a room.

Hanna came with him. The girls stayed at the library with Maria.

“You never brought me here before,” she said.

“I was afraid you would see it.”

“What?”

“How ordinary it was.”

The workshop had no hidden chamber. No perfect archive. No final machine waiting to reveal who had been responsible. It contained broken tools, unpaid invoices, and a wall where he had written equations he no longer understood.

Hanna touched the dust on a workbench.

“You thought the room would explain you.”

“I thought the machines knew what I had meant.”

“They did not?”

“They only did what I built them to do.”

He opened the old printer. The rollers were cracked. Inside, he found a fragment of paper with four words printed across it.

**REPAIR REQUIRES A WITNESS.**

He laughed softly.

“Did you write that?” Hanna asked.

“I don’t remember.”

The sentence could have been a message. It could have been an old note. It could have meant nothing.

He placed it on the workbench.

The broken machines did not need to become symbols.

They had already been useful.

Skyler took one last photograph of the room, then showed it to Hanna.

“Do you want to keep it?” she asked.

He looked at the image.

The photograph contained the printer, the dust, the broken sign, and two people standing in a room that had once held too much meaning.

“No,” he said.

He deleted it.

Outside, the girls were waiting with the new cards. They had added a machine to the deck.

“What does it mean?” Skyler asked.

“Something can be broken and still be useful,” Luna said.

“Can people?”

The girls looked at him.

Summer answered first.

“People are not machines.”

The correction was immediate.

Skyler nodded.

He carried the cards home without photographing them.
