# Long-Form Expansion Batch Forty-Three

## Chapter Seventy-Nine — The Interview

The interviewer wanted the chosen one.

She arrived at the library with a camera crew, a list of dramatic questions, and a producer who kept asking whether Skyler had a “final transformation.”

“What did you become?” the interviewer asked.

Skyler looked at the blank door behind her.

“Someone still becoming.”

“That is not a satisfying answer.”

“It is the accurate one.”

She tried another approach. “Were you chosen?”

“By whom?”

“By circumstance. By history. By the people who needed you.”

“Being needed is not the same as being chosen.”

“What is it, then?”

“A pressure placed on a person until they mistake endurance for destiny.”

The producer interrupted. “Can we make that more emotional?”

Skyler turned toward him.

“No.”

The interview continued without the dramatic arc the crew wanted. Skyler spoke about the phone, the campaign, the mistakes he had made, the people he had harmed, and the records that remained uncertain.

He did not call the Society all-powerful. He did not call himself innocent. He did not say the numbers or tarot cards were supernatural messages.

The interviewer asked whether he believed the manipulation had changed him.

“Yes.”

“For better or worse?”

“Both.”

The answer made the producer sigh.

After the interview, the interviewer stayed behind.

“You made my job difficult,” she said.

“I know.”

“I wanted a story about a man who survived the machine.”

“I survived parts of it.”

“That is less inspiring.”

“Maybe inspiration should be less interested in clean endings.”

She looked at the library volunteers, the open doors, the ordinary people reading records and waiting for appointments.

“What should the story be about?”

Skyler thought of the children’s card game, Maria’s private archive, Ghost’s front desk, Mara’s departure, and Hanna’s rule at home.

“About what people build after they stop believing one person can save them.”

The interviewer wrote it down.

The documentary aired a month later.

The title was **AFTER THE CHOSEN ONE**.

Skyler watched only the first ten minutes.

The film was imperfect. It contained a few compressed timelines and one dramatic voice-over he had not approved. It also showed the library, the people, and the long silences that had not fit the trailer.

He turned it off before the end.

The story no longer belonged to him.

That was not freedom.

It was the consequence of having released it.
