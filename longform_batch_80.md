# Long-Form Expansion Batch Eighty

## Chapter One Hundred Sixteen — The Correction That Turned Inward

The most difficult correction concerned the federation’s own name.

A local group discovered that a public report had overstated the number of people helped by the archive. The report had counted referrals as resolved cases and training attendance as consent to ongoing participation.

The mistake came from a spreadsheet created during the funding crisis.

No single person had invented the numbers. Everyone had accepted them because they made the work look successful enough to continue.

Mara found the discrepancy first.

She wanted to publish a correction immediately.

The board hesitated.

A public correction could damage donations, reassure critics, and cause communities to question whether the federation had exaggerated other results.

Skyler said, “Publish it.”

Noor asked, “Even if it closes two libraries?”

“If we keep it hidden, the libraries remain open on a story we know is false.”

The correction named the error without naming individual volunteers. It explained the difference between contact, participation, consent, and resolution. It listed the reports affected and promised a review of every public claim.

The response was immediate.

A donor withdrew.

A journalist called the federation fraudulent.

A community that had been counted as served sent a letter saying it had never agreed to be counted at all.

The federation removed the community’s name from the report and apologized without asking permission to publish the apology.

The board voted to stop using success totals unless every category had a documented definition and an audit trail.

The new reports looked smaller.

They were also more credible.

Skyler visited the two libraries threatened by the lost funding. Their workers were angry. One accused him of choosing honesty because he could survive the consequences better than they could.

He did not deny it.

“I have more protection than you,” he said. “That means I owe you more transparency, not less. It does not make the decision harmless.”

The worker stared at him.

“Then what do we do?”

“We decide together whether the work can continue without the claim.”

One library closed for three months and reopened through local donations. The other merged with a community center. Neither recovered its former budget.

The federation lost prestige.

It gained a rule that outlived the people who had made the mistake.

At the next public reading, Mara placed the corrected chart beside the original.

The two versions were almost identical in shape.

The difference was not visible until someone read the definitions.

That was how power often worked, Skyler thought.

The lie did not always require a different picture.

Sometimes it required only a softer word.
