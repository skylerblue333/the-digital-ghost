# Long-Form Expansion Batch Forty-Seven

## Chapter Eighty-Three — The Deletion Question

The deletion question divided the library.

A record contained a false accusation that had been disproven. The person named in it wanted the entire file destroyed. The archive policy preserved the original claim, the challenge, the evidence, and the correction.

The person said the policy was another injury.

The archivist said deletion would erase the history of how the injury happened.

Both were right.

Skyler asked for a private meeting with the person. She arrived with her brother and refused to sit near the archive wall.

“I do not want my name teaching anyone a lesson,” she said.

“You have the right to refuse public use.”

“Then delete it.”

“The policy preserves disputed records unless there is a safety reason to remove them.”

“I am the safety reason.”

The sentence ended the argument.

The council reviewed the policy. Imani warned that deletion could make institutions deny their past. Maria said preservation could make the archive another institution that valued history over people.

Noor proposed a two-part solution. The original record would remain in a sealed accountability archive, inaccessible to public search. The public record would contain only the correction and a note that an earlier harmful claim had been withdrawn.

The person could decide whether her name appeared in either place.

The compromise displeased everyone.

That was not proof it was right.

It was proof that the loss had been acknowledged.

The woman chose to remove her name from the public record. She allowed the sealed archive to retain the case without identifying details.

The policy changed.

**Preservation must not require continued exposure.**

Skyler wrote the sentence in the margin of the first Aegis refusal log.

The old system had treated memory as a permanent obligation.

The new system would have to learn that people were allowed to outgrow the evidence of what had been done to them.
