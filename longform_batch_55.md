# Long-Form Expansion Batch Fifty-Five

## Chapter Ninety-One — The Center Moves

The final community gathering of the year took place beneath the four doors.

People arrived with records, objections, cards, and questions. Some had followed the project since the church basement. Others had entered through the international glossary. A few had come only to challenge it.

Skyler stood near the back.

Noor opened the meeting.

She spoke about the Living Draft, the audits, the corrected records, and the people who had left. She did not mention Skyler until the end.

“We began with a man who believed he had to carry the whole story,” she said. “The work changed when he learned to put the story down.”

Skyler felt every face turn toward him.

He wanted to wave. He did not.

The meeting continued without a speech from him. People discussed the new access audit, the international adaptation, the sealed archives, and the deletion policy.

The project had become too large for one person to summarize.

That was the achievement.

At the close, Noor asked Skyler to join the council table.

He remained standing.

“I am not stepping away,” he said. “But I am stepping back.”

He proposed ending his permanent seat. His remaining term would expire at the end of the month. After that, he would be available as a public participant, subject to the same access rules as everyone else.

The room became quiet.

Hanna smiled from the doorway.

Lena looked startled. Ghost looked relieved. Imani, watching through a video call, nodded once.

Noor asked whether he was certain.

“No.”

“Then why do it?”

“Because certainty is not the requirement.”

The vote passed.

Afterward, the people gathered in the library courtyard. Someone played music from a small speaker. The children drew doors in chalk. Maria distributed bread. The security guard watched the entrance without standing between anyone and the street.

Skyler stood beside Hanna beneath the evening sky.

“What happens now?” she asked.

“I don’t know.”

She took his hand.

The answer no longer sounded like failure.

It sounded like space.
