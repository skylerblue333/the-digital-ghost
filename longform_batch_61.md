# Long-Form Expansion Batch Sixty-One

## Chapter Ninety-Seven — The Boundary That Remains

Sarah invited Skyler to lunch after the federation audit.

They met at a quiet restaurant where no one knew their names. The windows looked onto a street filled with bicycles and rain.

“You stepped down,” she said.

“I did.”

“People say that means you are healed.”

“I am not.”

She nodded. “Good.”

They ordered soup. The conversation moved around the girls, the library, the report, and the years neither of them could return.

Sarah asked whether he still kept the Truth Protocol notebook.

“Sometimes.”

“Do you write about me?”

“Only when you give permission.”

“Do you ask every time?”

“Yes.”

She looked relieved and sad at once.

“I used to think if I could make you understand what you did to me, you would stop doing it.”

“What changed?”

“I realized understanding is not control.”

The sentence belonged to both of them.

Skyler looked at the rain.

“I wanted you to confirm that I was chosen by circumstances,” he said. “That everything I did came from what happened to me.”

Sarah stirred her tea.

“Circumstances explain pressure. They do not choose your hands.”

He nodded.

They agreed on a new arrangement. They would speak about the past only when both consented. They would not use the girls as messengers. They would not ask the children to choose a version of the adults.

The boundaries were not forgiveness.

They were a structure in which forgiveness might someday become possible without being demanded.

At the end of lunch, Sarah gave Skyler a small envelope.

Inside was one page from the old calendar.

The page contained a date, a hospital admission, and a note in his handwriting:

**I WANT TO BE BELIEVED.**

On the back, Sarah had written:

**YOU WERE HURT. YOU WERE ALSO HERE.**

He folded the page and placed it in his wallet.

Not as proof.

As a reminder that a person could be both injured and responsible for what he did next.
