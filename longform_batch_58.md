# Long-Form Expansion Batch Fifty-Eight

## Chapter Ninety-Four — The Mentor

A young volunteer asked Skyler how to survive being misunderstood.

He almost answered with the story of his own life.

Instead, he asked, “What do you mean by misunderstood?”

The volunteer, Eli, said his family believed he was hiding something because he had stopped sharing his location. His employer believed he was unreliable because he refused a monitoring app. His friends believed he was paranoid because he kept asking who could see their group messages.

Skyler listened.

The details sounded familiar. That familiarity created danger. He wanted to tell Eli that the world was manipulating him. He wanted to tell him that fear could be evidence.

He remembered the people who had once listened to him and turned his uncertainty into a theory.

“What can you verify?” Skyler asked.

Eli looked frustrated. “I know how it feels.”

“I believe you.”

“Then why do you keep asking what I can prove?”

“Because feeling harmed and knowing the cause are different problems.”

Eli looked away.

Skyler helped him separate the issues. His employer had required a monitoring app. That policy was documented. His family had been asking for location data. That conversation was real. His friends had made jokes about him. That was painful, but it did not establish coordination.

They drafted a boundary message and a request for the employer’s privacy policy. They did not name a secret organization. They did not turn every coincidence into a sign.

Eli returned the following week.

“The message worked,” he said.

“Good.”

“My family still thinks I’m hiding something.”

“They may.”

“How do I make them understand?”

“You may not be able to.”

The answer was not comforting.

It was freedom from another impossible task.

Eli sat quietly.

“Did anyone teach you this?” he asked.

“People taught me by letting me see where I was wrong.”

“Were you grateful?”

“Not at first.”

The volunteer smiled.

Skyler did not call himself Eli’s mentor. He did not add the meeting to his public biography. He wrote only the process in the training notes, with Eli’s permission and no identifying details.

Experience could be useful without becoming rank.

That was another door he had not known he needed.
