# Long-Form Expansion Batch Forty-One

## Chapter Seventy-Seven — The Last Explanation

Thorne requested one final meeting through the public defender.

He was not asking for a deal. He wanted to give testimony about the Society’s architecture before the case moved beyond his control.

Skyler met him in a courthouse conference room with no windows. The room contained a table, six chairs, and a clock that ran four minutes slow.

“You will want a complete explanation,” Thorne said.

“I want a record.”

“Those are different things.”

Thorne described the Society’s origin. It had begun as a private arbitration network for companies that could not agree on reputational risk. The early members believed they were preventing panic. They shared information, built predictive models, and created informal rules for who deserved access to which markets.

Aegis made the process efficient.

The Choir made it persuasive.

The Ledger made it profitable.

The Mirror Room made it personal.

No single founder had intended the final system. Every participant had contributed a reasonable decision until the accumulated structure became unreasonable.

“Who was in charge?” Skyler asked.

“Everyone who benefited.”

“That is evasive.”

“It is incomplete, not evasive.”

Thorne named directors, contractors, investors, journalists, and analysts. Some had left. Some had died. Some had become respected leaders in other institutions.

The Society had no final chamber where all the villains sat together.

It had habits that moved through people.

“What did you do?” Skyler asked.

Thorne looked at the slow clock.

“I refined the system.”

“Why?”

“Because I was good at it.”

The answer was smaller than Skyler expected.

Not ideology. Not destiny. Skill rewarded until reward became justification.

“Did you believe you were chosen?” Skyler asked.

Thorne smiled without humor.

“No. I believed I was useful. Chosen is what people call usefulness after they become afraid of losing it.”

Skyler thought of the title he had carried, the one other people had placed on him and the one he had secretly enjoyed. The Chosen One. The person capable of saving the others. The person whose suffering made his judgment special.

The title had been another room.

Thorne slid a folder across the table.

Inside was a list of systems that had adopted Society methods without using its name.

“Publish this?” Skyler asked.

“Only if you can prove it.”

“Why give it to me?”

“Because you still want a final enemy.”

Skyler closed the folder.

“I want responsibility.”

“No,” Thorne said. “You want responsibility to have a face.”

The meeting ended without reconciliation.

Outside, Skyler stood beneath the courthouse clock. The real time differed from the clock by four minutes.

A small error. An ordinary error.

He did not correct it.

He let time remain imperfect in public.
