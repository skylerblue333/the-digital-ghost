# Long-Form Expansion Batch Sixty-Two

## Chapter Ninety-Eight — The Closed Meeting

The federation’s first external attack came through a forged invitation.

A private meeting was announced under the federation’s name. The invitation asked members to submit confidential records before attending. The address belonged to an empty office.

Several groups nearly complied.

Noor called an emergency meeting.

The representatives argued over whether the federation should stop publishing its calendar and move all coordination into a private channel.

The proposal was reasonable.

It was also the first step toward becoming what they feared.

Skyler said nothing until the room asked him to choose.

“Secrecy can protect people,” he said. “It can also remove the witnesses who make authority answerable.”

A representative from the international group replied, “That is easy to say from a city where the police recognize your organization.”

Skyler accepted the criticism.

“Then the rule cannot be identical everywhere.”

They created a layered system. Public meetings would remain public where safe. High-risk groups could hold private sessions, but the existence, purpose, and decision rules of those sessions had to be recorded. No private meeting could create a permanent policy without public review.

The arrangement frustrated everyone.

It protected no one completely.

It prevented secrecy from becoming invisible.

The forged invitation was traced to a marketing contractor that denied knowing the federation existed. The evidence was incomplete. The group published a warning without naming the contractor.

A journalist accused them of cowardice.

The representative from the international group replied, “We are not obligated to provide a name so that you can feel the story has an ending.”

The sentence spread farther than the warning.

That evening, Skyler stood outside the library while Noor locked the front door.

“Did we do the right thing?” she asked.

“I don’t know.”

“You say that often.”

“It is often true.”

She looked through the glass at the four doors.

“Maybe the point is not knowing.”

“Maybe the point is showing what we did while we did not know.”

They left the lights on in the entrance.

The building closed.

The record remained visible.
